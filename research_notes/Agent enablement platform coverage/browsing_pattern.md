# Browsing-pattern access: how AI shopping agents that drive a merchant's own storefront are identified, blocked, and enabled (as of 3 November 2026)

Scope note: "browsing pattern" here means an agent that drives the merchant's human-facing web storefront (search, PDP, cart, checkout) either in a cloud browser (Muse, Instinct, Grok Bot, ChatGPT agent/Cloud browser, Claude Duet, Comet-in-cloud, Gemini Spark remote mode) or inside the user's own browser (Comet local, Claude in Chrome, Gemini in Chrome auto browse, Edge Copilot Mode Actions, ChatGPT desktop browser). It excludes protocol/feed checkout (ACP/UCP/Instant Checkout) except where needed for comparison. All claims dated; "vendor claim" vs "measured" flagged where the distinction matters.

## Key Question 1: Identification — which browsing agents send identifiable signatures, which are indistinguishable, and what Amazon's "does not identify itself" complaint means technically

### Takeaway
As of early November 2026 only a small set of cloud-hosted agents (OpenAI's ChatGPT agent/Cloud browser, Google-Agent for a subset of Google infrastructure, Manus) sign requests with Web Bot Auth (RFC 9421 HTTP Message Signatures); every in-browser agent (Comet local, Claude in Chrome, Gemini in Chrome, Edge Copilot Actions) and the main cloud consumer agents (Meta Muse, Instinct, Grok Bot) present as ordinary Chrome with no agent token, so sites can at best infer them by fingerprint. Amazon's complaint against Muse is literally that Muse does not put "Agent/[name]" in its User-Agent string as Amazon's August 14 2026 Conditions of Use require; DataDome found Meta quietly began signing a "relatively small" share of Muse/Meta-WebIndexer requests with Web Bot Auth around 12 October 2026, unannounced.

### Cited Findings
- A 26 October 2026 test that sent 23 agents to a site logged user-agent strings: ChatGPT's desktop-app browser, Claude in Chrome, Comet (both in the user's browser and in Perplexity's cloud), Grok, Claude computer use, Browser Use and Playwright MCP all presented stock Chrome UAs with `navigator.webdriver` false and no signature; Manus was the only agent that both carried a UA token (`Manus-User/1.0`) and signed requests with Web Bot Auth; Claude Cowork and Vercel agent-browser were detectable only via `HeadlessChrome` UA / `webdriver=true`; Copilot in Edge produced no request in the test; Gemini in Chrome could not be tested — [dev.to, Quentin Wendegass, 27 Oct 2026](https://dev.to/quentin_wendegass/we-sent-23-ai-agents-to-our-website-most-looked-human-3a7e)

#### Standards status
- Cloudflare "signed agents" launched August 2025 as an extension of its verified-bots program; launch cohort included ChatGPT agent, Block's Goose, Browserbase, Anchor Browser — [Cloudflare blog, Aug 2025](https://blog.cloudflare.com/signed-agents/)

### Inferences
- The identification split is architectural, not policy: cloud-hosted agents can sign (and OpenAI, Google, Manus, and now a sliver of Meta do), but in-browser agents inherit the user's UA, cookies and residential IP, so no header-based standard can identify them; the only levers are fingerprinting (fragile, as Perplexity's 24-hour evasion and Muse's Firefox fallback show) or action-level controls at login/checkout.
- "Does not identify itself" means two different things: Amazon's contractual UA-string rule (which Muse fails) and the cryptographic Web Bot Auth rule (which Muse is just beginning to meet for a small share of traffic, without publishing a key directory). Even full signing identifies the platform, not the user — WorkOS and Centinel both note that a signature says nothing about which user or whether they are authorized.
- Visa TAP has processors and CDNs on the verification side but no documented consumer shopping agent enrolled; adoption evidence is confined to a B2B pilot.

### Gaps
- No primary Meta documentation of Muse's identification or of the Web Bot Auth test; only DataDome's observation via PPC Land. Meta's 20 Nov 2026 crawler-terms change is unconfirmed in a Meta source.
- Amazon's Conditions of Use page returned 503; Agent Terms wording is from search excerpts and secondary coverage. The CAPTCHA-solving prohibition cited by some outlets was not confirmed in the official text.
- No measured count of agents enrolled in Visa TAP or merchants validating TAP signatures; no evidence of Muse, Comet, Instinct, Grok Bot, Claude, or Gemini being TAP-registered.
- No Cloudflare-published count of signed agents; the only figure (95 AI bots in Radar as of 1 Oct 2026, 67 with identifiable UA) is third-party and unverified.
- Grok Bot: no detection-vendor profile or identification documentation found. "Fo": no agent by that name found in any source.
- Gemini in Chrome and Edge Copilot Actions: no vendor statement on identification; evidence is absence of documentation plus a vendor assessment.

## Key Question 2: Blocking landscape — who blocks, who allows, what "accidental" blocking means, and how CDN/bot-manager defaults treat agents

### Takeaway
Measured data points converge on a web that mostly has not decided: Billions Network's August 2026 audit of 51 large US retailers found 5 explicit restrictions, 37 "accidental" blocks via legacy robots.txt rules on account/checkout paths, and zero verified-agent purchase routes; Akamai found commerce sites left >90% of AI-bot traffic in monitor-only mode (H2 2025); HUMAN's measured block rate on agentic traffic rose from 8.2% (April 2026) to 21% (June 2026), and HUMAN blocks Muse by default. Cloudflare's September 15 2026 new-domain defaults block "Agent" traffic on ad-bearing pages, and non-verified bots are blocked by default under its AI traffic categories.

### Inferences
- "Accidental" blocking has at least three distinct mechanisms the sources describe separately: (a) legacy robots.txt disallows on /cart, /checkout, /account that are advisory and only bite agents that honor robots.txt (Billions); (b) bot-manager defaults that block non-verified automation (Cloudflare: non-verified blocked by default; HUMAN: Muse blocked by default; Akamai: checkout scored for automation); (c) human-verification interstitials that a cloud agent cannot or will not pass (Walmart). Cloud agents like Muse are hit by (b) and (c), while in-browser agents are mostly untouched by all three because they ride the user's session.
- Counts of blocking retailers are unreliable: the only audit (Billions) measures stated policy, not enforcement, and has a commercial interest; HUMAN/Akamai/DataDome measure traffic on their own customer bases, which skew toward sites that bought bot management.
- On Shopify/WooCommerce/Wix no platform-wide "agents blocked" default was found; Shopify's robots.txt line is a comment, its checkout bot protection applies to unsigned agents, and WooCommerce/Wix publish MCP endpoints rather than blocks.

### Gaps
- The September 2026 HUMAN block-rate percentage and the June/September report bodies could not be fetched (403); July/August rates are unreported in what was retrieved.
- No measured share of Shopify, WooCommerce or Wix stores that block browsing agents; no DataDome/Kasada per-platform data. Kasada published no 2026 agent-blocking data that was found.
- The 2 retailers Billions says are "starting to welcome" agents into checkout are unnamed.
- Cloudflare publishes no figure for how many zones block the "Agent" category.

No test documented variant-picker, address-form, or shipping-calculator failures specifically — the agents mostly did not get that far, or those steps were absorbed by Shop Pay/Link.
- The only quasi-completion metric (Forter approval 44%→73%) measures fraud-decisioning, not navigation success, and the jump reflects Forter relaxing thresholds, not agents improving.

### Gaps
- No published end-to-end completion rate for any browsing agent on any storefront; CNN, NBC, and BI originals were partially or fully inaccessible (451, interstitial, not located).
- No test evidence on variant selection, shipping calculators or stale page data as failure causes; DaVinci's catalog-data complaint (missing texture) is the closest.
- Forter has not published chargeback/fraud rates by agent type (deferred to a webinar).

## Key Question 4: Agent-readable storefront — llms.txt, JSON-LD variants, feed specs, WebMCP, and whether any of it improves browsing-agent success

### Takeaway
"Agent-readable" infrastructure exists mainly at the discovery layer (llms.txt now on most Shopify stores by platform default; Google-compatible product feeds for OpenAI/Google/Meta/Microsoft) and, since 28 September 2026, at the action layer on Shopify (Checkout WebMCP with Web Bot Auth) and on ~100,000 Commerce.com domains via Cloudflare edge-injected WebMCP (cart only; checkout link handed to the shopper). No source provides measured evidence that llms.txt or schema markup improves a browsing agent's checkout success; the Billions audit notes three llms.txt publishers (Pottery Barn, West Elm, Williams-Sonoma) that still block checkout.

### Cited Findings
- llms.txt adoption: 8.7% of the top 1,000 sites (June 2026), e-commerce 8.5% (10/118) — [Rankability](https://www.rankability.com/data/llms-txt-adoption/); 5.61% of top 10,000 with Shopify at 78.1% because Shopify pushed llms.txt by default in late April–early May 2026 — [Casey Burridge](https://caseyrb.com/blog/state-of-llms-txt-adoption/); BuiltWith counts >7.3M live sites serving llms.txt, nearly matching live Shopify stores — [Project LLMs.txt](https://projectllmstxt.com/blog/llms-txt-adoption-2026)
- Pottery Barn, West Elm and Williams-Sonoma publish llms.txt "but all three still block checkout" — [Billions Network, 6 Oct 2026](https://billionsnetwork.substack.com/p/how-top-us-retailers-are-responding)
- Shopify Checkout WebMCP (28 Sep 2026): tools `navigate_to_storefront`, `get_checkout`, `update_checkout`, `complete_checkout`; "don't expose a new API or require merchant configuration"; agents must sign with Web Bot Auth (Ed25519 key, hosted key directory registered with Shopify); excluded: three-page checkout unless Shop Pay, B2B, embedded/mobile SDK checkouts, multi-shop carts, draft orders; Chromium-only (Chrome 149 origin trial); WebMCP is a W3C Community Group draft — [Shopify changelog, 28 Sep 2026](https://shopify.dev/changelog/webmcp-support-for-checkout); [Shopify dev docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/carts-and-checkout/checkout-webmcp); [TechCrunch, 28 Sep 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/shopify-opens-checkout-to-browser-based-ai-agents/); [Stellagent summary](https://stellagent.ai/insights/shopify-webmcp-checkout-browser-agents)
- Commerce.com + Cloudflare (21 Oct 2026): WebMCP on "almost 100,000" domains via one zone-level setting; tools include `search_products`, `add_to_cart`, product details, and get a checkout link; "the shopper clicks checkout personally to finish buying"; merchant opt-out via dashboard switch setting `webmcp_opt_out` hostname metadata enforced by a Cloudflare Configuration Rule; no agent identification described; no usage or completion metrics — [Cloudflare blog, 21 Oct 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/webmcp-commerce/)
- Cloudflare's edge WebMCP (script injection, no code) entered beta in August 2026; "Markdown for Agents" also offered — [Cloudflare blog, 21 Oct 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/webmcp-commerce/); [Cloudflare blog, 30 Sep 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/agentic-web)
- Wix gives every site a built-in, unauthenticated MCP server at `/_api/mcp` letting visitor agents browse products and start a checkout; payment completes on the store's checkout page — [UCP Checker](https://ucpchecker.com/blog/wix-ucp-guide-ai-agent-commerce)
- WooCommerce MCP shipped in 10.3 (Oct 2025) for product/order management with a stated goal of eventually powering consumer checkout; still labeled developer preview/beta as of 10.7 — [WooCommerce developer blog, 3 Oct 2025](https://developer.woocommerce.com/2025/10/03/ai-agentic-commerce-in-woocommerce/); [Your Next Store roundup](https://yournextstore.com/blog/mcp-servers-for-ecommerce)
- Vercel BotID (botid@1.5.0+) returns `isVerifiedBot`, `verifiedBotName`, `verifiedBotCategory` from Vercel's verified-bot directory, so a checkout route can admit `chatgpt-operator` and 403 everything else; Vercel warns gating on `isBot` alone rejects signed agents; its AI-bots managed ruleset is inactive by default — [Vercel docs](https://vercel.com/docs/botid/verified-bots); [Vercel ACP page](https://vercel.com/i/agentic-commerce-protocol); [Vercel guide](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/agentic-commerce-readiness). A "Vercel Shop" (19 Oct 2026) MCP storefront for Shopify merchants is reported only by a third-party blog — [blog.mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/vercel-news-october-2026/)
- OpenAI's product feed spec accepts Google-style feeds, one product or variant per row, falls back to `id` as group when no grouping field, normalizes color/size into `variant_dict`, supports updates as often as every 15 minutes, delivered via API or SFTP by application — [OpenAI commerce spec](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/spec); [Marpipe comparison](https://www.marpipe.com/blog/product-feed-specs)
- Google's structured-data variant model uses `ProductGroup` with `variesBy` and nested `hasVariant` Products each with its own offer — [DEV guide](https://dev.to/arnaud_buisine_eea153b88a/a-practical-guide-to-json-ld-product-schema-for-ai-shopping-agents-11k5)
- Shopify reports (18 Jun 2026) AI-driven traffic to Shopify stores up 8x YoY in Q1 2026, orders from AI searches up ~13x, AI-channel new buyers ordering at ~2x other channels (vendor data, covers protocol/channel traffic, not browsing agents specifically) — [Shopify blog, 18 Jun 2026](https://www.shopify.com/blog/how-agentic-commerce-works)

### Inferences
- The one place where "agent-readable" translates into browsing-agent *checkout* is Shopify Checkout WebMCP, and it only helps agents that (a) run in a Chromium browser, (b) implement WebMCP, and (c) register a Web Bot Auth key — which today excludes Muse, Instinct, Grok Bot and every in-browser agent that doesn't sign. The DaVinci test's "only because it was Shopify" result is consistent with Shop Pay wallet integration, not WebMCP.
- Feed specs (OpenAI/Google/Meta/Microsoft) serve the protocol pattern; they do not alter what a browsing agent sees on the storefront, so variant-level JSON-LD quality matters for browsing agents only insofar as the agent reads page markup — and Muse reads the accessibility tree, not JSON-LD, per Botify.

### Gaps
- No measured evidence (A/B or observational) that llms.txt, JSON-LD completeness or WebMCP raises browsing-agent completion; vendor readiness frameworks are unvalidated.
- No independent audit of variant-level JSON-LD quality on retailer sites.
- Shopify has not disclosed Checkout WebMCP eligibility criteria, regions, agent partners, or how agent orders appear in admin.
- No "MCP for my store" vendor was found with published browsing-agent success data.

## Key Question 6: Browsing versus protocol — share of agent-completed orders and quality differences

### Takeaway
Forter's network data (the only order-level dataset found) shows screen-scraping agents going from about 1% of agentic orders in late July 2026 to 77% in the week of 19 October, driven by Muse (49% of agentic orders that week); scraping agents authorize at 98.8% versus 100% for protocol agents but carry higher merchant cancellation rates, needed a 24 September fraud-threshold relaxation to lift approval from 44% to 73%, and give merchants no verified buyer identity. These are vendor-network figures without published methodology.

### Cited Findings
- Forter classification: protocol agents = OpenAI (ChatGPT Instant Checkout) and Google, identified by referral source; screen-scraping agents = Muse, Instinct, Grok Bot, ChatGPT Agent, identified by agent platform, driving the merchant's own checkout pages — [Forter, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.forter.com/blog/ai-agent-order-data/)
- Share: scraping agents ~1% of agentic orders in late July, 77% in week of 19 Oct 2026; OpenAI placed 80–92% of agentic orders each week in August; Muse 49% of agentic orders week of 19 Oct and >50% in its first detected week; Muse placed 7.3x as many orders in its first 39 days as Google did in 13 weeks; through 7 Oct Muse bought from 74% of stores receiving any agentic order, ChatGPT Agent from 45% (nearly double OpenAI's protocol rate); all agentic orders up 7.2x since first week of August — [Forter, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.forter.com/blog/ai-agent-order-data/). (An earlier Forter post cited 70% for the week of 12 Oct — [Forter, agentic checkout post](https://www.forter.com/blog/ai-assistants-agentic-checkout/))
- Quality: authorization 100% protocol / 98.8% scraping / 98.9% humans; approval for scraping agents 44%→73% after 24 Sep threshold change with no material change for humans or protocol agents; customer cancellations 1–2% for humans and agents except Google (10%); merchant cancellations highest for ChatGPT Agent and Instinct, lowest for OpenAI, ~10% for humans; protocol agents have lower cancellation rates than scraping agents; merchant cancellations of scraping-agent orders 27% in Asia/Middle East and 37% in Europe; average agentic order $227, Google's 56% below average and single-item only — [Forter, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.forter.com/blog/ai-agent-order-data/)
- Forter's qualitative contrast: protocol agents deliver "structured, verified order data"; scraping agents "look like a shopper clicking through the site" and merchants cannot see the real buyer — [Forter, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.forter.com/blog/ai-agent-order-data/)
- Forter press claims (vendor): agentic orders up >4x since August; automated fraud doubled in two months; no dollar baselines — [Forkast, 20 Oct 2026](https://forkast.news/the-holiday-fraud-test-is-weeks-away-and-the-agent-stack-isnt-ready/)
- Traffic-side corroboration: e-commerce was 76.5% of Muse traffic and 60.4% of all agentic traffic in September 2026 (HUMAN); 8.9% of sampled Muse requests touched forms/login/payment/cart (DataDome via Vouched) — [HUMAN September 2026 (excerpt)](https://www.humansecurity.com/learn/blog/september-2026-agentic-traffic-triples-meta-muse-debuts-at-no-1/); [Vouched](https://www.vouched.id/learn/blog/muse-is-an-agent-but-it-doesnt-want-you-to-know)
- Meta says shopping is Muse's top use case with more than 25% of users shopping (Zuckerberg); Meta will take a "small fee"; Muse topped the App Store free chart — [CNBC, 3 Oct 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/03/meta-muse-shopping-ai-agent.html); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ai-agent-muse-shop-e-commerce-amazon-wal-mart-wayfair-buy-rcna599069)
- OpenAI moved away from Instant Checkout toward retailer apps inside ChatGPT (March 2026), which is context for why its protocol share fell — [CNBC, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html)

### Inferences
- Browsing became the dominant agent order path in under three months not because it works better but because Muse's distribution (App Store #1, Stripe Link virtual cards) routes orders through merchants' existing checkouts without any merchant integration — exactly the path merchants cannot verify, which is why fraud vendors had to loosen thresholds to stop rejecting it.
- The quality gap is in verification and cancellation, not card authorization: scraping agents' cards authorize nearly as often as humans', but merchants cancel more of their orders, especially outside North America.

### Gaps
- The "nearly 98% of agentic orders linked to a person" claim attributed to Forter was not found in Forter's own posts or the Forkast article; treat as unverified.
- No non-Forter order-level dataset; no Shopify, Stripe, or Meta disclosure of Muse order counts or completion rates.
- Forter does not report chargeback or fraud rates by agent type; detection dates lag actual first orders.
