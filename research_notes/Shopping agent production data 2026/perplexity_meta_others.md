## Meta Muse — new data since mid-October 2026

### Takeaway
The only new hard numbers since mid-October are Meta's own Q3 call claims (21 Oct 2026: 10M+ set-ups in US/Canada in ~6 weeks, ~25% daily use among set-ups, shopping used by >25% of weekly actives with average purchase >$200) plus Sensor Tower's download/DAU estimates; no order counts, accuracy, cancellation or dispute data were disclosed by Meta or any merchant beyond DoorDash's "several hundred" (29 Oct). Journalist tests continue to find stalled checkouts and payment-selection confusion rather than completed wrong orders.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Meta's ">25% of weekly actives shop" and ">$200 average purchase" cannot be converted into order counts because Meta gave no WAU figure; DoorDash's "several hundred" total across three agents is the only merchant-side order count and implies Muse order volume outside Meta-partner connectors is tiny.
- The Q3 retention claim (25% of set-ups use daily) and Sensor Tower's 23% day-14 retention are broadly consistent; the Hunterbrook/Morgan Stanley signals point to decelerating new-user growth rather than a retention collapse.
- Product design (approval card, single-use Link cards, planned spend caps, prompt rules to reopen links and not guess sizes) indicates Meta is engineering against wrong-order and wrong-price failure modes rather than claiming they are solved.

### Gaps
- No Meta or merchant disclosure of Muse order counts, order accuracy, cancellation, return or dispute rates in the window.
- No statements from Walmart, Wayfair, Best Buy, Gap or Sephora quantifying Muse-originated orders; Wayfair reports Q3 on 4 Nov 2026 (not yet available).
- No Sensor Tower retention update dated after ~20 Oct 2026 was found.
- No Verge, WSJ, Washington Post, Wirecutter or Tom's Guide structured shopping-list test of Muse was found.

## Independent hands-on tests and third-party measurement (May–Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
No major-outlet structured "shopping list, count right vs wrong" test of a production agent was found in the window; the closest are journalist spot tests of Muse (CNN, NBC, Business Insider) that mostly ended in stalled or blocked checkouts, and academic/vendor studies showing agents' product picks are highly unstable (Wharton) and chatbots' shopping answers are wrong 86% of the time (Product.ai).

### Cited Findings
- Wharton (Kumar et al., reported 27 Aug 2026; SSRN 7355899): six models (Claude Opus 4.8, Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5.5, GPT-5 Mini, Gemini 3.5 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite) choosing a fitness watch in the ACES simulator; a single external source (e.g., a Wirecutter review) shifted selection probability by up to 90–99 percentage points; more sources increased variability; no wrong-item/overpay rates reported — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-shopping-agents-arent-ready-to-buy-on-your-behalf-study-finds/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7355899)
- Product.ai (reported ~late Sept 2026): ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity gave conflicting or incorrect answers to 220 shopping questions 86% of the time; wrong prices were off by a median of $300 (Q&A, not purchases) — [Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/ai-shopping-assistants-not-ready-holidays)
- Unverified: a forum repost describes a 600-trial study of Operator, Project Mariner, Perplexity Shopping Agent and a WebVoyager baseline with a 48% failure rate (wrong items, overpaying, fabricated pages); no primary source, authors or venue could be located — [Gnoppix forum](https://forum.gnoppix.org/t/ai-shopping-agents-arent-ready-to-buy-on-your-behalf-study-finds/7194)
- Business Insider (22 Oct 2026): Muse/Instinct/Dot lunch-order test; Muse surfaced an HSA-linked card at payment selection; no order submitted (details in Muse section) — [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.co.uk/meta-muse-hsa-card-ai-shopping-test-1809348)
- CNN (23 Sept 2026) and NBC News (29 Sept 2026) Muse tests: blocked at Amazon, manual checkout at Target, stalled shampoo order, closed venues recommended — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/23/tech/meta-muse-ai-agent); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ai-agent-muse-shop-e-commerce-amazon-wal-mart-wayfair-buy-rcna599069)
- Billions Network audit of 51 large US retailers (reported 1 Nov 2026): 5 explicitly restrict agents, 37 block by accident, none offers a verified agent purchase path — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/01/jpmorgan-amazon-ai-agents-meta-muse.html)
- BACKGROUND (Mar 2026): Adobe data cited by nShift: AI-referred traffic converted 42% better than non-AI in March 2026 vs 38% worse in March 2025 (referral traffic, not agent checkout); nShift's own read is that agent-completed checkout "did not become the norm in 2026" and the common pattern is recommend-then-hand-off — [nShift](https://nshift.com/blog/ai-shopping-agentic-inversion-2026)
- BACKGROUND (Feb 2026 McKinsey survey, n=4,008, via Retailgentic 22 Mar): 23% of consumers say they use AI agents to check out and buy — [Retailgentic](https://www.retailgentic.com/p/5-agentic-commerce-news-items-big)
- Analyst/expert explanations for low conversion (1 Nov 2026): Accenture's Michael Abbott argues blockers lose long term; SharpLink's Joseph Chalom expects incumbents to keep gates closed because agents threaten switching costs and margins; Princeton's Ed Felten expects blocking to fade; Wharton's Ethan Mollick warned of a "perfectly legal distributed denial of service attack" from consumer agents on customer-service departments — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/01/jpmorgan-amazon-ai-agents-meta-muse.html)

### Inferences
- The best-sourced evidence in the window says agent purchases fail mostly at the access/checkout layer (blocking, bot checks, license gates, payment-method confusion) rather than through completed wrong orders, which is consistent with Walmart, Meta/Stripe and Anthropic all defaulting to human approval.
- Survey-claimed agent checkout usage (23%) is irreconcilable with DoorDash's observed "several hundred" orders, suggesting consumers conflate AI-assisted search with autonomous purchasing.

### Gaps
- No Wirecutter, The Verge, WSJ, Washington Post, Tom's Guide, Marketplace Pulse or Retailgentic structured right-vs-wrong shopping-list test of Muse, Perplexity, Duet, Instinct or Fo was found for May–Nov 2026.
- The 48%/600-trial study could not be traced to a primary source; do not cite its figures without one.
- No academic field study of a production agent's order accuracy in the window was found.
