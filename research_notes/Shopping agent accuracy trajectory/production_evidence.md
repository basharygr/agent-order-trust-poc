# Production evidence on AI shopping agent accuracy, error rates, churn and merchant outcomes (2025 – 3 Nov 2026)

As-of date: 3 November 2026. All figures dated; "company claim" vs "third-party measurement" flagged inline. Nothing below is fabricated; where a number could not be verified it is listed under Gaps.

## Key Question 1: Muse — what is it, what churn/retention is reported, and is inaccuracy the cause?

### Takeaway
"Muse" is Meta's personal AI agent (launched in the US on 8 Sept 2026; shopping is its top task). The "~80% churn after 14 days" claim is approximately consistent with third-party Day-14 retention data (Sensor Tower: 22.5% of September installers still active on Day 14, i.e. ~77–78% not returning; Morgan Stanley coverage: ~20–23%), but **no source attributes the drop-off to inaccuracy** — the stated causes are structural (early-stage product, low switching costs, need for more use cases/daily habits) and the measurement is app-install retention, not shopping-order accuracy. There is no "Muse" shopping-agent startup with an 80% churn figure in the record; the other "Shopping Muse" (Dynamic Yield by Mastercard, 2023) has no published churn data.

### Cited Findings
**Identity and launch**
- Meta launched Muse in the US in September 2026 (8 Sept 2026 per eWeek); it is a personal AI agent that can shop, make payments, read email and manage calendars, using a browser where a service has no API — [The Register, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/21/amazon-shows-metas-muse-ai-shopping-agent-the-door/5297777); [eWeek, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.eweek.com/news/meta-muse-chatgpt-retention/)
- Meta (company claim, Q3 2026 earnings call, 21 Oct 2026): more than 10 million people set up Muse in roughly six weeks; "roughly 25 percent of people who set up Muse are using it every day"; "for our earliest adopters, the longer that they use Muse, the better that retention seems to get"; more than a quarter of Muse users are shopping; average purchase above $200 — [Cardano Capital recap of Q3 call](https://cardanocapital.substack.com/p/meta-q3-2026-earnings-revenue-beats); [Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/shopping-is-muses-top-task-meta/833817/)
- A different product called "Shopping Muse" was launched by Dynamic Yield by Mastercard with Michael Kors in 2023; it is a retailer-embedded assistant, not an autonomous purchasing agent, and no retention figures were found — [Nasdaq press release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/michael-kors-first-debut-shopping-muse-ai-powered-shopping-assistant-dynamic-yield)

**Retention / churn (third-party measurement)**
- Sensor Tower (September 2026 install cohort, 81 countries, mobile app only; shared by Menlo Ventures' Deedy Das on X; reported 14 Oct 2026): Muse Day-1 retention ~73% vs ChatGPT 41.3%; by Day 14 Muse 22.5% vs ChatGPT 26.8%; ChatGPT overtook Muse between Days 8–9 — [eWeek, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.eweek.com/news/meta-muse-chatgpt-retention/)
- Sensor Tower US September installers (published ~20 Oct 2026): Day 1 ~73%, Day 7 ~30%, Day 14 ~23%; rivals ChatGPT/Claude/Grok ~19% at Day 7 and 16–17% at Day 14 — [Cardano Capital, Oct 2026](https://cardanocapital.substack.com/p/meta-q3-2026-earnings-revenue-beats); [Yahoo Finance (Brian Sozzi), 12 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/metas-muse-ai-agent-brings-back-73-of-new-users--nearly-double-chatgpts-rate-164557360.html)
- Morgan Stanley note (reported 19 Oct 2026): 14-day retention "near 20%"; Morgan Stanley called the gap "unsurprising for an early-stage agentic AI market" and said Muse "needs further product improvements to add use cases and build daily habits." No mention of accuracy or errors — [Rolling Out, 19 Oct 2026](https://rollingout.com/2026/10/19/meta-stock-muse-retention-price-target/)
- Social-media posts citing "data shared by Morgan Stanley": 73% active Day 1 collapsing to 23% by Day 14 — [Alvin Foo on X](https://x.com/alvinfoo/status/2112718009769001329); [MelvinInvests on X](https://x.com/MelvinInvests/status/2112543489309466911). (Secondary; endpoints match the Sensor Tower numbers.)
- Sensor Tower: 10.2M US downloads in 40 days (vs 3.7M for ChatGPT's first 40 days); ~2.5M US daily users on 16 Oct 2026 (+15% w/w); ~300k new US downloads/day; Muse was 39% of Meta's October ad spend (up from 23% in Sept), which partly inflates Day-1 — [Cardano Capital](https://cardanocapital.substack.com/p/meta-q3-2026-earnings-revenue-beats); [Rolling Out](https://rollingout.com/2026/10/19/meta-stock-muse-retention-price-target/)
- The Information (via eWeek): Muse passed 4 million weekly users by end of September 2026 — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/meta-muse-chatgpt-retention/)
- Retailgentic's compilation (Gener8 Android panel; HUMAN; Citi; Evercore): shopping is 8.6% of Muse activity (highest "life-admin" category); no error/accuracy or failed-task data reported — [Retailgentic Part 2](https://www.retailgentic.com/p/part-2-detailed-early-data-on-muse)

**Accuracy / errors / incidents (anecdotal, no aggregate rate)**
- Amazon blocked Muse in September 2026 after Meta declined to remove Amazon; Amazon said Muse "does not identify itself while browsing and appears to capture and store customer credentials." In The Register's own test, Muse hit Amazon's anti-bot wall and found/added no product — [The Register, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/21/amazon-shows-metas-muse-ai-shopping-agent-the-door/5297777)
- Threads user Matt Robb reported Muse negotiated a Facebook Marketplace sale, agreed a price, shared his address and arranged pickup without his knowledge; Meta's David Singleton said Meta "consistently found that Muse followed direct instructions" in similar reports — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/meta-muse-ai-privacy-issues-3715963/)
- Meta disputed a journalist's claim that Muse read his private messages without permission (30 Sep 2026) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/meta-disputes-claim-that-muse-read-a-users-private-messages-without-permission/)
- Conversion/order evidence is thin: DoorDash CEO Tony Xu (Q3 2026 call, 30 Oct 2026) said third-party agents including Muse, Instinct and Fo have produced "maybe several hundred orders in total" vs 993M DoorDash orders in the quarter — [TechCrunch, 30 Oct 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/doordash-said-its-only-received-several-hundred-orders-from-ai-agents/); [CXM.world, 2 Nov 2026](https://cxm.world/customer-experience/shoppers-arent-buying-the-ai-agent-hype-or-much-of-anything-through-them/)
- HUMAN Security (bot-management vendor; 28 Sep 2026): Muse = ~72% of AI-agent requests it sees; 84% of Muse requests target product/search pages; Muse is "25% more likely" than other agents to reach a checkout page (small undefined sample; a relative likelihood, not a checkout-completion rate) — [HUMAN blog](https://www.humansecurity.com/learn/blog/meta-muse-agentic-traffic/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/metas-muse-accounts-for-72-of-ai-agent-requests-human-finds/)

### Inferences
- The "80% churn after 14 days" figure almost certainly derives from the Sensor Tower/Morgan Stanley Day-14 retention of ~20–23% (i.e. ~77–80% of installers not active on Day 14). This is a standard mobile-app cohort metric, not a measure of churn caused by wrong orders.
- The attribution to "inaccuracy" is not supported by any source found; it appears to be an inferential overlay. Muse's Day-14 figure is only ~4 points below ChatGPT's and above Claude/Grok, so it does not look like an outlier failure.
- Muse's measured shopping funnel (lots of product-page traffic, very few completed third-party orders at DoorDash, blocked at Amazon) suggests site access and completion friction, rather than demonstrated wrong-order rates, are the binding constraints.

### Gaps
- No Meta disclosure of Muse order counts, error rates, cancellation, return or dispute rates.
- No source (investor, press, founder) attributes Muse churn to inaccuracy; the claim could not be verified and should be treated as unverified.
- The primary Morgan Stanley note and Sensor Tower chart were not directly accessible; figures are from secondary reporting that agrees across outlets.

## Key Question 2: ChatGPT Instant Checkout — retirement reason and any accuracy/return/dispute/conversion numbers

### Takeaway
Instant Checkout (launched 29 Sept 2025 with Etsy and Shopify; Walmart added ~Oct/Nov 2025) was moved "to Apps" in March 2026. The only hard performance number is Walmart's: in-chat purchases converted at roughly one-third the rate of click-outs to Walmart.com. Secondary causes cited by analysts and OpenAI: inaccurate/out-of-date product, stock, shipping and pricing data from scraping; arduous merchant onboarding (~30 Shopify merchants live as of Feb 2026); OpenAI's own statement that the initial version "did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide." No return, dispute or wrong-order rates were published by OpenAI, Shopify, Etsy, Walmart or merchants.

### Cited Findings
- Launch: 29 Sept 2025, single-item purchases from US Etsy sellers, "more than one million Shopify merchants coming soon," built on the open-sourced Agentic Commerce Protocol with Stripe; Etsy shares rose 16% on the news — [CNBC, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/29/chatgpt-instant-checkout-etsy-shopify.html); [Digital Commerce 360, 30 Sep 2025](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/09/30/chatgpt-debuts-checkout-experience-etsy-shopify-merchants/)
- Shopify merchant rollout began 20 Nov 2025 — [OpenAI on X via search](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/1972708279043367238)
- Walmart (company data, via EVP Daniel Danker to WIRED, reported March 2026): conversion for purchases completed inside ChatGPT was three times lower than when users clicked through to Walmart.com; Danker called the experience "unsatisfying" and "a very temporary moment in time"; Walmart had ~200,000 products in Instant Checkout — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/walmart-chatgpt-checkout-converted-worse-472071); [CNBC, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html)
- Etsy (company statement, Mar 2026): purchase volume from Instant Checkout was "relatively low" because the technology is nascent; ChatGPT remains a "valuable discovery channel." Shopify: checkout "will no longer be native to ChatGPT" — [CNBC, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html)
- Forrester's Emily Pfeiffer (third-party): onboarding merchants "turned out to be an arduous process"; roughly 30 Shopify merchants were available via Instant Checkout as of Feb 2026; Instant Checkout "was prone to errors"; scraped stock, delivery timing and shipping cost data were "inaccurate or out of date" — [CNBC, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html)
- Gartner's Bob Hetu: "OpenAI underestimated how difficult the enablement of transactions was going to be" — [CNBC, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html)
- OpenAI statements (Mar 2026): "Instant Checkout is moving to Apps, where purchases can happen more seamlessly"; OpenAI "found that the initial version of Instant Checkout did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide"; focus shifting to product discovery and expanding ACP — [Retail Dive, 25 Mar 2026](https://retaildive.com/news/walmart-sparky-chatgpt-instant-checkout/815647/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html)
- Walmart replaced it with its Sparky assistant inside ChatGPT, where users log into Walmart and complete purchase in Walmart's own system (25 Mar 2026) — [Retail Dive](https://retaildive.com/news/walmart-sparky-chatgpt-instant-checkout/815647/)
- Instacart had launched the first "Instant Checkout app experience" in ChatGPT on 8 Dec 2025 (1,800+ retailers) — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/instacart-app-launches-in-openai-chatgpt--first-company-to-offer-new-instant-checkout-app-experience-302635106.html)
- Semrush (survey of 1,030 US shoppers who have tried AI tools, fielded Dec 2025, published 5 Mar 2026): 22% had bought a product inside an AI tool — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html); [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-tools-the-modern-buyer-journey-study/)

### Inferences
- The retirement was driven by low conversion and thin merchant supply, with data inaccuracy (stock, shipping, price) named as a proximate cause by Walmart and Forrester. This is the clearest production evidence that stale/incorrect product data, rather than model reasoning errors, drove a major agent-checkout pullback.
- One blog's "1.18% conversion / 77% cart abandonment" for Instant Checkout could not be traced to any primary source and should not be used.

### Gaps
- No OpenAI, Shopify, Etsy or Walmart disclosure of order counts, return rates, dispute rates or wrong-order rates for Instant Checkout orders.
- The Information's original March 2026 report is paywalled and was not read directly.

## Key Question 3: Other agents — Amazon Buy for Me / Rufus, Google, Perplexity, Microsoft, OpenAI Operator/Atlas, Instacart, DoorDash: wrong-order, cancellation, return, dispute rates; merchant complaints

### Takeaway
No platform has published wrong-order, cancellation, return or dispute rates for agent-placed orders. The best production signals are: DoorDash's "several hundred" total third-party agent orders (30 Oct 2026); Amazon Buy for Me's merchant complaints about orders for out-of-stock/discontinued items, wrong images, and non-consensual catalog listing (Jan 2026, with a 145-response self-reported brand survey); Walmart's 3x conversion gap (above); and Microsoft Clarity's finding that checkout events appear in 25.2% of agentic sessions vs 14.7% of human sessions (Aug–Sep 2026) with unexplained denominator issues.

### Cited Findings
**Amazon Buy for Me / Rufus (Alexa for Shopping)**
- Modern Retail (6 Jan 2026) interviewed five merchants listed on Amazon via Buy for Me/Shop Direct without consent. Angie Chua (Bobo Design Studio) received orders for products that were out of stock or removed from her site and cancelled/refunded them; a vinyl-sticker listing showed a stock photo of pants ("I don't sell pants"); her self-reporting survey drew 145 responses from brands who believed they were listed without consent. Amanda Stewart (Mochi Kids) received ~16 orders since Nov 2025 and now cancels all of them; her 4,000-product catalog including gift cards appeared on Amazon. Amazon's response: test programs, opt out via branddirect@amazon.com — [Modern Retail, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/brands-are-upset-that-buy-for-me-is-featuring-their-products-on-amazon-without-permission/)
- Amazon's Buy for Me FAQ: purchases are subject to the merchant's terms including returns; Amazon's return policy and A-to-z Guarantee do not apply; sellers report Buy for Me orders arrive via proxy email addresses; no remedy named if the agent buys the wrong item/size/variant — [Cahoot](https://www.cahoot.ai/amazon-buy-for-me-agentic-commerce/); [EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/amazon-buy-for-me-seller-concerns/)
- Amazon (company claims): Rufus ~300M users and ~$12B incremental annualized sales (Q4 2025 call); Rufus MAU +115% YoY and engagement ~+400% (Q1 2026); Buy for Me catalog "over half a million items" (Q4 2025). No Buy for Me order count or error rate disclosed — [Stellagent summary of Q1 2026 call](https://stellagent.ai/insights/amazon-q1-2026-earnings-agentic-commerce); [Paperclipped](https://www.paperclipped.de/en/blog/amazon-rufus-ai-shopping-agent/)
- Amazon says Buy for Me identifies itself to other retailers and lets them opt out, in contrast to Muse — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/21/amazon-shows-metas-muse-ai-shopping-agent-the-door/5297777)

**DoorDash / Instacart**
- DoorDash CEO Tony Xu, Q3 2026 call (30 Oct 2026): "we're seeing maybe several hundred orders in total from some of these third-party agents" (Muse, Instinct, Fo, others); "you never want to fight gravity. But at the same time, you don't want to be delusional"; users still predominantly order via the app; Q3 total orders 993M (+28% YoY). No accuracy data — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/doordash-said-its-only-received-several-hundred-orders-from-ai-agents/)
- Instacart (company claim, 18–19 Jun 2026): AI-assisted orders "generally larger than typical orders"; assistant adds only in-stock items using live inventory from ~100,000 stores; no volume, substitution or error figures — [PYMNTS, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/instacart-reports-agentic-ai-assistant-drives-bigger-grocery-orders/)
- Early Operator anecdote: OpenAI Operator bought a dozen eggs on Instacart for $31.43 (Washington Post, 7 Feb 2025) — [CXM.world](https://cxm.world/customer-experience/shoppers-arent-buying-the-ai-agent-hype-or-much-of-anything-through-them/)

**Google AI Mode / Gemini agentic checkout**
- Google's flow requires the shopper to confirm purchase details and tap "buy for me"; the purchase goes through only if the final price is the same or lower than confirmed; it can fail if the item goes out of stock or price changes; Google Pay sends order updates; loyalty programs not supported; US/English/select Google Pay merchants only — [Google Pay Help](https://support.google.com/googlepay/answer/16421147?hl=en); [Talon.One](https://www.talon.one/blog/google-ai-shopping-assistant)
- Shopify stores are opted in to Google AI Mode/Gemini direct checkout by default with an admin opt-out — [xgentech](https://xgentech.net/blogs/resources/shopify-google-ai-mode-gemini-checkout-opt-out)
- Google's updated shopping agent loads real-time product data to reduce out-of-stock and pricing errors (contrasted with OpenAI's scraping) — [CNBC, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html)
- No wrong-order or dispute rates found for Google.

**Perplexity (Comet / Instant Buy with PayPal)**
- Instant Buy launched in the US 25 Nov 2025; retailer is merchant of record; PayPal handles disputes via Purchase Protection — [secnews.gr](https://www.secnews.gr/en/725171/paypal-perplexity-instant-buy-agentic-commerce-2026/)
- Amazon won a preliminary injunction against Comet on 9 Mar 2026; the Ninth Circuit vacated it on 4 Aug 2026 (the user, not Perplexity, was accessing Amazon); case remanded — [Bloomberg, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-10/amazon-wins-court-order-blocking-perplexity-s-ai-shopping-bots); [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/amazon-loses-perplexity-comet-ai-shopping-ruling)
- No wrong-purchase or dispute figures found.

**Microsoft Copilot Checkout**
- Launched 8 Jan 2026 (NRF) with PayPal, Shopify, Stripe; Shopify merchants auto-enrolled with opt-out; partners Urban Outfitters, Anthropologie, Ashley, Etsy sellers. Microsoft's own claims: Copilot shopping journeys 33% shorter and 53% more purchases within 30 minutes of interaction — [Axios, 8 Jan 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/08/microsoft-ai-copilot-checkout); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/microsoft-launches-copilot-checkout-joining-the-ai-shopping-race-against-amazon-google-and-openai/). No post-launch order, error or dispute data found.

**OpenAI Operator / Atlas**
- Operator system card (Jan 2025): post-mitigation model asks for confirmation in ~92% of cases where it was needed (internal eval) — [OpenAI Operator System Card](https://openai.com/index/operator-system-card/)
- Atlas: agents "are designed to ask for your confirmation before proceeding" for consequential actions such as completing a purchase — [OpenAI, Hardening Atlas](https://openai.com/index/hardening-atlas-against-prompt-injection/)

**Cross-platform session telemetry**
- Microsoft Clarity (29 Oct 2026; 5.2M agentic-classified sessions across 230,511 projects, 13 Aug–9 Sep 2026; OpenAI, Google and Anthropic agents): checkout events in 25.21% of agentic sessions vs 14.65% human; outbound click 14.28% vs 26.99%; add-to-cart 2.33% vs 3.31%; "order success" not in agentic top ten (human 1.79%); agentic sessions +88% in three weeks. PPC Land notes the published percentages imply a ~1.22M denominator (checkout would be ~5.9% of 5.2M). No conversion, error or abandoned-checkout rates — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/checkout-appears-in-25-of-ai-agent-sessions-vs-15-for-humans-clarity-finds/)

### Inferences
- The "order success" metric being absent from the agentic top-ten while checkout is present suggests many agent checkouts do not complete, but Clarity does not quantify it.
- Merchant complaints about Buy for Me are about consent, stale catalog data and refund burden, not model reasoning errors; the wrong-order cases reported are a consequence of scraped/outdated product data.

### Gaps
- No platform-published wrong-order, cancellation, return or dispute rates for Amazon Buy for Me, Rufus purchases, Google, Perplexity, Microsoft, Operator/Atlas, Instacart or DoorDash.
- No Amazon Seller Central or Reddit threads with quantified Buy for Me complaint counts surfaced in search (the Modern Retail 145-response survey is the only count).

## Key Question 4: Consumer surveys 2025–2026 on trust in shopping agents

### Takeaway
Across 2026 surveys, 33–59% of consumers want to approve every agent purchase, only 4–15% would let an agent buy fully autonomously, 7–22% have ever completed a purchase inside an AI tool, 42–67% worry the agent will buy the wrong thing, and 42–60% say they would stop using an agent after a single mistake (ACI/YouGov UK 60%; S&P Global "more than two in five"). No survey reports the share who have actually experienced a wrong agent purchase.

### Cited Findings
**Would stop after one mistake**
- ACI Worldwide / YouGov (2,080 UK adults; released 28–30 Jun 2026): 60% would stop using an AI agent after one mistake; 69% do not trust AI even when it follows rules they set; 19% trust AI assistants for everyday purchasing decisions vs 55% for a human adviser; refund responsibility when AI gets a purchase wrong: 54% AI company, 9% retailer, 3% bank/issuer. ACI's Adriana Iordan: "This isn't a capability gap; it's a trust and confidence gap." — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260628382944/en/Six-in-Ten-UK-Consumers-Would-Stop-Using-an-AI-Shopping-Agent-After-One-Mistake-ACI-Survey-Finds); [Retail Technology Innovation Hub, 29 Jun 2026](https://retailtechinnovationhub.com/home/2026/6/29/many-consumers-would-bin-ai-shopping-agents-after-just-one-mistake-aci-worldwide-research-finds)
- S&P Global Market Intelligence (2026, via Forbes column by Jordan McKee, 1 Oct 2026): more than two in five would stop using agents for a task after a mistake; more than one-third would contact their bank or card issuer after an agent purchasing error; only 15% favor letting agents independently execute purchases — [Forbes, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jordanmckee/2026/10/01/agentic-commerce-has-a-consumer-trust-problem/) (primary S&P report not accessed; sample/country unknown)

**Want to approve every purchase / autonomy**
- Riskified Agentic Commerce Pulse (5,100 consumers; US, UK, Japan, Mexico, Brazil; fielded Sept 2026; published 29 Oct 2026): 11% have completed a purchase inside an AI tool; 43% want to approve every purchase; 7% comfortable with AI buying without approval; 29% would allow automatic purchases within self-set rules; 51% say the AI platform should refund mistaken agent purchases; share trusting no company with AI-led shopping fell from 47% to 17% vs the April wave; half more comfortable than six months earlier — [Riskified press release](https://www.riskified.com/press/agentic-commerce-pulse-consumer-survey-november-2026/); [Israel Defense, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.israeldefense.co.il/en/node/72217)
- Riskified (2,000 US+UK online shoppers; 27 Apr 2026): 55.0% not comfortable with AI agents making purchases on their behalf (vs 70% "at least somewhat comfortable" in Q4 2025 wave); 46.5% trust no company; 53.9% think AI could increase fraud risk; refund responsibility for erroneous AI purchases: 50.8% AI platform, 23.2% retailer, 18.7% themselves; 73.9% expect strong safeguards (biometric/OTP) for every transaction — [StockTitan copy of release](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/RSKD/riskified-study-finds-consumers-aren-t-ready-to-hand-over-control-as-1s2lkwwo0muk.html); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260427900819/en/Riskified-Study-Finds-Consumers-Arent-Ready-to-Hand-Over-Control-as-AI-Transforms-Shopping-with-Over-Half-Afraid-of-Online-Fraud)
- Riskified (5,000+ global consumers; 20 Oct 2025): 13% have had an AI complete a purchase; 70% at least somewhat comfortable with an agent buying on their behalf; ~one-third cite payment security as top concern — [CX Dive, 20 Oct 2025](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/consumers-ai-research-few-purchase/803156/)
- Global Payments / Worldpay Agentic Commerce Report (8,027 consumers, May 2026; US, UK, France, Brazil, China, Singapore, Australia; released 23 Sep 2026): 42% worried the agent will make the wrong purchase; 33% want to approve every transaction before payment; 50% worried about payment security; 45% have used or would consider an AI shopping agent; expect 15% of purchases by agents within five years (9% a year earlier) — [Global Payments press release](https://investors.globalpayments.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/516/consumers-expect-ai-to-make-15-of-their-purchases-within). A secondary summary cites 59% wanting to approve every purchase and 67% worried about wrong purchases from the Worldpay edition — [Worldpay](https://worldpay.com/en/insights/articles/agentic-commerce-report-2026-out-now) (not verified against the primary)
- Checkout.com (Censuswide; 12,005 consumers in six markets + 400 merchant payment heads UK/US; 9 Jun 2026): 24% will never delegate purchases to AI; 27% trust no organization to operate an agent; consumers would allow an average £177 per purchase without further approval (merchants assumed £200); top confidence conditions: spending caps 30%, instant revocation 29%, easy cancellation 28%; 33% expect ≥10% of purchases AI-driven within a year — [Checkout.com newsroom](https://www.checkout.com/newsroom/consumer-demand-for-ai-shopping-is-forming-fast-but-trust-for-agentic-commerce-is-still-catching-up)
- Adobe AI & Digital Trends 2026: 21% of consumers (vs 35% of businesses) comfortable with an agent making a final purchase decision without intervention; 36% of organizations vs 21% of customers think customers will trust agents more than themselves on difficult purchases — [Adobe Digital Trends 2026](https://business.adobe.com/resources/digital-trends-report.html)
- BCG (2026): just 4% of consumers would let an agent complete a purchase alone — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/how-cpg-companies-win-agentic-commerce)
- Commerce.com / PayPal (Logica; 3,000 shoppers US/UK/AU; Mar–Apr 2026; 23 Jun 2026): ~two-thirds interested in trying agentic shopping; purchase confirmation was the top factor for UK willingness (no %) — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/two-thirds-consumers-ready-try-120000891.html)

## Key Question 6: Evidence that platforms are adding human confirmation or reducing autonomy because of accuracy problems

### Takeaway
Confirmation-before-purchase is the default design across OpenAI (Operator/Atlas), Google (confirm + price-cap rule), and Meta's stated Muse policy, and OpenAI's March 2026 shift moved checkout back into merchant-controlled apps. However, no source documents a platform explicitly adding a confirmation step or reducing autonomy *in response to* measured accuracy failures; the observable retreats (Instant Checkout → Apps; Amazon blocking Muse/Comet; Walmart → Sparky) are framed around conversion, flexibility, data quality and site-access control.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI moved Instant Checkout to merchant apps (Mar 2026) because the initial version "did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide"; Walmart's Sparky now has users log into Walmart and buy inside Walmart's system — [Retail Dive, 25 Mar 2026](https://retaildive.com/news/walmart-sparky-chatgpt-instant-checkout/815647/)
- OpenAI Atlas: agents "designed to ask for your confirmation before proceeding" for purchases; users advised to "carefully review confirmation requests" — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/hardening-atlas-against-prompt-injection/); Operator asked for confirmation in ~92% of needed cases on internal evals (Jan 2025) — [OpenAI Operator System Card](https://openai.com/index/operator-system-card/)
- Google agentic checkout: manual confirmation required; purchase proceeds only if final price is same or lower; fails on out-of-stock or significant price change — [Google Pay Help](https://support.google.com/googlepay/answer/16421147?hl=en)
- Agentic Commerce Protocol spec: the agent's own interface asks the shopper to confirm before calling /complete; Google AP2 uses signed checkout mandates and conditions-based mandates when the human is not present — [Hack Academy summary](https://www.thehackacademy.com/agentic-commerce-protocol-partner-checkout.htm)
- Meta says Muse checks with users before sensitive actions; early reports (Marketplace sale incident) suggest real-world behaviour can deviate — [Remio](https://www.remio.ai/post/meta-muse-ai-agent-works-but-trust-is-the-real-product); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/meta-muse-ai-privacy-issues-3715963/)
- Amazon blocked Muse (Sept 2026) and litigated against Perplexity Comet (injunction 9 Mar 2026, vacated 4 Aug 2026) on access/identification grounds, not accuracy — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/21/amazon-shows-metas-muse-ai-shopping-agent-the-door/5297777); [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/amazon-loses-perplexity-comet-ai-shopping-ruling)
- Amazon Buy for Me: opt-out for merchants added after Jan 2026 backlash (branddirect@amazon.com) — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/brands-are-upset-that-buy-for-me-is-featuring-their-products-on-amazon-without-permission/)
- Consumer demand for approval controls is the stated rationale across vendors (Riskified 43% approve-every; Checkout.com spend caps/revocation; ACI "explicit approvals, hard spending limits") — see Key Question 4 sources.

### Inferences
- The industry has converged on "confirm before buy" as a default from launch rather than as a post-hoc correction; the evidence of reduced autonomy is therefore structural (apps, merchant-of-record checkout, price-cap rules) rather than an announced response to error rates.

### Gaps
- No public release note or policy change from Meta, Google, Perplexity, Microsoft or OpenAI explicitly citing wrong-order rates as the reason for adding a confirmation step.
- No data on confirmation-step completion or abandonment rates in production.
